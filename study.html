Study 001 / P002Version 1 · 2 October 2026

What does
“non-progression”
mean?

A bounded audit of spinal structural outcomes in axial spondyloarthritis trial reports.

Completed descriptive pilot / Internally checked / Not externally validated

Choose your starting point

For patients and the general public

Plain-language abstract

Study initiated: 1 October 2026 (protocol draft)
Study finalized: 2 October 2026 (version 1)

Background and hypothesis
Does “no progression” mean nothing changed in the spine? Some trial definitions allow a small increase in an imaging score. We asked how definitions affect the reported results and whether they distinguish preventing new damage from repairing existing damage. This was a descriptive question, rather than a formal test of a hypothesis.
Methods
We examined accessible reports published from 1 January 2018 through 2 October 2026 about adults with axial spondyloarthritis and spinal X-rays roughly two years apart. We recorded the score cutoffs, people included, image-reading methods and handling of missing information. Where possible, we compared different cutoffs within the same group of participants. AI workers cross-checked key numbers internally.
Results
The audit contains 46 endpoint records from nine trial families, with five matched comparisons from four families. In one MEASURE-1 dataset, 60.7% of participants counted as non-progressors when no score increase was allowed, compared with 82.1% when an increase of up to two points was allowed. The 21.4 percentage-point difference comes from rounded published rates. The cutoff changed; this comparison did not show a new treatment effect. Some reports shared image-reading work, and one comparison relied on an inferred link between its participant counts.
Conclusions
A “non-progression” result needs its definition and study context. The audit provides a guide and reusable records for interpreting these results. It does not rank medicines or demonstrate repair of existing damage. The search and source access were limited, some checks remain incomplete, and there has been no external expert validation. The inspected material contained no endpoint specifically measuring repair of an existing lesion; that does not mean repair is impossible.

For researchers and physicians

Structured abstract

Study initiated: 1 October 2026 (protocol draft)
Study finalized: 2 October 2026 (version 1)

Background and hypothesis
Radiographic “non-progression” in axial spondyloarthritis depends on the outcome definition and does not establish reversal of existing structural damage. We asked how thresholds and analysis populations affect reported non-progression and whether inspected outcomes distinguish prevention, measurement variability and repair. This was a descriptive research question, not a confirmatory hypothesis test.
Methods
We conducted a bounded audit of accessible reports published from 1 January 2018 through 2 October 2026, covering adult axial spondyloarthritis randomized trial families and related reports with nominal 96–112-week spinal radiographic outcomes. One screener assessed 42 Europe PMC title/abstract records, supplemented by additional discovery. Extraction preserved populations, exact thresholds, readers, denominators and missing-data methods. We calculated within-cohort threshold contrasts where analysis conditions were judged comparable and internally cross-checked pivotal values using AI workers. No effects were pooled.
Results
Nine parent trial families contributed 46 endpoint records; four families supported five within-cohort contrasts. In the MEASURE-1 comparative reading dataset, changing the modified Stoke Ankylosing Spondylitis Spine Score (mSASSS) change threshold from ≤0 to ≤2 changed reported non-progression from 60.7% to 82.1%: a 21.4 percentage-point difference from rounded published rates. Definitions differed across contrasts. MEASURE-1 and ENRADAS shared a reading campaign; SELECT-AXIS-1’s denominator linkage was inferred. No lesion-specific repair endpoint was identified in the inspected material.
Conclusions
Non-progression proportions need to be read alongside their threshold, population and analysis method. The source-linked dataset supports interpretation and reuse; the contrasts describe classification differences, not treatment benefits or structural repair. This was not a comprehensive systematic review. Selection and access bias, incomplete registry histories and supplement/status checks, and absence of external expert validation limit inference. No treatment ranking or claim of novelty is supported.

Reading the result

Stability is not
the same as repair.

A measured score can remain below a progression threshold without showing that an existing lesion has regressed. Repair requires a different outcome: follow the same existing lesion and establish that it changed in the direction specified before the study.

No lesion-specific repair endpoint was identified in the structural material inspected for these nine families. That scoped finding does not establish that repair is impossible or that it has never been studied. Scope of the public record →

03 / Methods

How the audit
was assembled.

  1. Define a manageable scope.

    Accessible reports published from 1 January 2018 through 2 October 2026, with randomized parent trials or their extensions/secondary reports and nominal 96–112-week spinal mSASSS or syndesmophyte outcomes. Actual image windows were recorded separately; a nominal visit does not ensure the same imaging interval across studies.

  2. Search and preserve the limits.

    One EuropePMC title/abstract query returned 42 records, screened by one lab screener. Supplemental discovery added known reports outside that count. An initial 841-result all-fields query was preserved but not screened. This was not a complete systematic review.

    Exact 42-record query
    (TITLE_ABS:"axial spondyloarthritis" OR TITLE_ABS:"ankylosing spondylitis") AND (TITLE_ABS:mSASSS OR TITLE_ABS:"radiographic progression" OR TITLE_ABS:syndesmophyte*) AND (TITLE_ABS:trial OR TITLE_ABS:randomized OR TITLE_ABS:randomised) AND FIRST_PDATE:[2018-01-01 TO 2026-10-02]

    Retrieved 2 October 2026. Eight records were marked include, four companion, two potentially eligible but access limited, two companion but access limited, and 26 excluded/background/outside-report-scope. These are record dispositions, not unique trial-family counts.

  3. Extract what makes a result interpretable.

    Preserve the population, exact inequality, imaging readers, missingness, estimator, nominal week, denominator and source location. When an exact analysis denominator could not be confirmed, it remained unknown. For SURPASS, baseline imaging counts were not relabeled as endpoint analysis counts.

  4. Challenge before calculating.

    Separate AI workers checked pivotal source entries they had not extracted, with orchestrator adjudication. This was not duplicate independent screening or complete independent re-extraction of every field, and it was not external expert validation. Comparisons with uncertain population or estimator linkage were excluded.

  5. Make only the supported calculation.

    Five matched within-cohort contrasts were retained. No common effect was pooled and no drugs were ranked. Registry histories, some supplements and publication-status checks remained incomplete; the bounded release amendment makes these unfinished components explicit.

04 / Limits

What remains
unanswered.

This pilot is an incremental interpretation dataset. It does not establish novelty, comprehensive coverage, comparative treatment efficacy or clinical significance of the numerical differences.

Coverage. A limited single-database search, single screening and accessible-source selection can omit relevant evidence. POSTURE, BE-MOBILE-2 and the pooled COAST-V/W report remained access limited.

Comparability. Imaging windows, patient populations and outcome rules differ. MEASURE-1 and ENRADAS are separate parent cohorts but not independent methodological replications.

Review. Correlated AI checking is a useful error-catching step, not qualified external scrutiny. Historical registration concordance and full supplement/status review remain unfinished.

What would change the result. A source correction, or a hidden difference between paired populations, images or estimators, would require withdrawing and recalculating the affected contrast.

Our proposed checklist for a future repair study

Methodological proposal, not a validated outcome standard.

  1. Define the existing lesion precisely; distinguish squaring, syndesmophytes and mature bridges.
  2. Identify the same lesion on comparable baseline and follow-up images.
  3. Prespecify lesion-level regression, separate from prevention or a composite-score decrease.
  4. Blind readers, quantify disagreement and prespecify adjudication.
  5. Track missing participants, images and scored locations separately.
  6. Distinguish measurement error from biological change and group-effect uncertainty.
  7. Seek corroboration and qualified external imaging/methods review before a strong repair claim.
