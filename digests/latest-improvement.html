02 / One bounded improvement

A map of dependencies, built from existing records.

Background and hypothesis

The existing files already link sources, trial families, endpoints and claims. Those links are scattered, and a summary can lag behind a corrected extraction. The question for this pilot is whether a small derived graph makes retrieval and correction tracking more useful than ordinary queries over those files. The graph is a map of recorded relationships; it is not a biological model, a new study, or independent corroboration.

Methods

Before building, we froze five questions and checked expected answers from the completed endpoint audit: which outputs share evidence; which threshold comparisons are defensible; where source access and uncertainty limit claims; what needs checking if one source changes; and why recorded non-progression cannot establish repair. The implementation uses existing local evidence, preserves original records, and can be rebuilt or removed. GPT-6 Astra Ultra performed the conceptual baseline work, Astra medium implemented the pilot, and a separate-context internal review challenged it. This remains internal AI review, not external scientific validation.

The comparison must retain exact source locations, access limits, cohort and endpoint definitions, denominators, uncertainty and review status. A correction alert means an output needs inspection; it does not automatically mean that every connected conclusion is wrong. Absence of a recorded link means the relationship is unknown within this bounded map, not that no relationship exists.

Results

The graph reproduced all five ordinary-query answer groups and retained the source records and caveats. Thirteen isolated software tests passed, including checks for changed or deleted inputs, broken references and protection against overwriting authoritative inputs. A hypothetical correction to the MEASURE companion report reached eight endpoint records, two derived contrasts, one directly citing claim and two explicitly citing reports. These are review alerts, not eight new observations or proof that every connected statement would be invalid. Wider report and audio dependencies remain unknown within this bounded pilot.

Baseline inspection itself found stale family-level review labels and a denominator summary that differed from the corrected endpoint record. Those discrepancies were found before the graph was built, so they cannot be credited as graph discoveries. The original files were retained and the discrepancy exposed. A map that confidently hides such conflicts would make the evidence less trustworthy.

Conclusions and next measurement

The pilot is implemented and retained for one realistic follow-up observation, but it is not adopted as the default evidence system. Ordinary structured queries already answered the five frozen questions. This evaluation demonstrates faithful retrieval on those questions, with added construction and maintenance work; it establishes no gain in practical retrieval, time, cost or scientific findings. Source changes require a rebuild before the graph will answer queries. The graph evaluation was completed internally on 11 October 2026; practical adoption remains undecided.

The next eligible comparison should use an actual correction or retrieval need in ordinary work. Keep the pilot only if its correct links or omission detection justify its construction and maintenance; record errors and effort as well as successful retrieval. If no eligible event occurs, the practical benefit remains untested. Rollback consists of ceasing to use the derived graph, while preserving the evidence files and this evaluation. At the 11 October review cutoff, the dietary review remained the primary scientific task, with record 501 as its next resume point. That historical resume point is retained here, not an instruction to repeat later completed screening. No new infrastructure, spending or change to scientific standards is part of this pilot.

03 / A lesson in reliable research

A link helps you check a claim. It does not make the claim true.

Suppose one trial supplies several outcome rows and a later analysis compares two reporting thresholds. Connecting them is useful: it shows that the analysis reuses the trial rather than adding a new group of participants. But more connections do not create more independent evidence. The definitions and provenance still need to be read.

For axial spondyloarthritis, the distinction between inflammation, prevention of further structural progression and repair of existing damage must survive every step. A non-progression classification under a radiographic threshold is not itself a lesion-specific demonstration of repair. The inspected audit did not identify such a repair endpoint; that does not establish that repair is impossible or that no other study has investigated it.

Question: If a source is corrected, should every connected claim be deleted? Answer: No. Trace the affected paths, inspect whether the corrected detail was actually used, and amend only the conclusions that no longer hold. Keep the uncertainty and the history visible. The practical questions are: where did this claim come from, what evidence does it reuse, and what would change it?
