A checked correction with no recorded ordinary use

The lab's correction preview remains available and internally checked, but the work recorded since its creation does not establish an ordinary use of it. Today's single improvement action was to measure that exposure and stop short of another extension. Practical benefit is still unmeasured; the preview has not failed an effectiveness test.

Why this was the useful next question

On 22 October, the previous improvement review (SI006) created a separate preview of corrections to wording in the lab's earlier audit of clinical-trial outcomes, project P002. That made thirteen previously reviewed source decisions inspectable in the saved files without overwriting the original release. Internal comparisons and selected deliberate faults checked that implementation. They did not establish whether anyone would subsequently need the preview, whether it would improve a scientific decision, or whether its upkeep was worthwhile.

The next review therefore needed to ask whether the change had encountered the problem it was intended to help solve. Creating another version before answering that question would add work without an identified consumer. We selected one modest measurement: classify the subsequent completed work against that use question. We deferred repairing the original file-generation script, correcting six text fields with unresolved character-display problems, maintaining the evidence graph and adding new verification machinery.

The resulting decision is to retain the reviewed preview without expansion. This follows the lab's existing genuine-need rule; no new department, schedule, approval gate or standing maintenance function was introduced. It preserves a useful source-backed artifact while leaving the current research priorities visible. The expected benefit is a more proportionate next decision, not a measured saving in time or money.

A research lesson: an observation needs the right opportunity

Suppose an instrument is designed to detect a particular signal. If no suitable signal reaches it during an observation period, the record cannot establish how well it detects that signal. The same distinction applies to a research workflow: a reproducible correction can be technically sound while its usefulness remains unknown because later tasks asked different questions.

This matters when interpreting SpA studies too. Before drawing a conclusion from an absent event or a percentage, establish what was observed, in whom, for which outcome and over what opportunity. A starting patient count does not automatically supply the denominator of every estimated result. The earlier denominator lesson explains the checked example and links its source. Today's workflow observation supplies no evidence about treatment efficacy, inflammation, progression or structural repair.
