What does “non-progression” mean?

A closer look at how trials describe spinal structural change in axial spondyloarthritis.

Completed descriptive pilot / Internal checks / No external validation

Read the full report ↗Explore the data guide →

Choose your reading view

Plain-language abstract

Study initiated: 1 October 2026 (protocol draft)
Study finalized: 2 October 2026 (version 1)

Background and hypothesis

Does “no progression” mean nothing changed in the spine? Some trial definitions allow a small increase in an imaging score. We asked how definitions affect the reported results and whether they distinguish preventing new damage from repairing existing damage. This was a descriptive question, rather than a formal test of a hypothesis.

Methods

We examined accessible reports published from 1 January 2018 through 2 October 2026 about adults with axial spondyloarthritis and spinal X-rays roughly two years apart. We recorded the score cutoffs, people included, image-reading methods and handling of missing information. Where possible, we compared different cutoffs within the same group of participants. AI workers cross-checked key numbers internally.

Results

The audit contains 46 endpoint records from nine trial families, with five matched comparisons from four families. In one MEASURE-1 dataset, 60.7% of participants counted as non-progressors when no score increase was allowed, compared with 82.1% when an increase of up to two points was allowed. The 21.4 percentage-point difference comes from rounded published rates. The cutoff changed; this comparison did not show a new treatment effect. Some reports shared image-reading work, and one comparison relied on an inferred link between its participant counts.

Conclusions

A “non-progression” result needs its definition and study context. The audit provides a guide and reusable records for interpreting these results. It does not rank medicines or demonstrate repair of existing damage. The search and source access were limited, some checks remain incomplete, and there has been no external expert validation. The inspected material contained no endpoint specifically measuring repair of an existing lesion; that does not mean repair is impossible.

Spinal radiographic non-progression in axial spondyloarthritis: a descriptive audit of trial endpoints

Study initiated: 1 October 2026 (protocol draft)
Study finalized: 2 October 2026 (version 1)

Background and hypothesis

Radiographic non-progression in axial spondyloarthritis is defined using heterogeneous thresholds. We aimed to characterize these definitions and quantify within-cohort differences in reported non-progression proportions across thresholds.

Methods

We conducted a descriptive audit of accessible randomized-trial reports published from 1 January 2018 to 2 October 2026, including extensions and secondary analyses with nominal 96–112-week spinal radiographic outcomes. Forty-two Europe PMC records underwent single screening, supplemented by targeted retrieval. Extraction captured endpoint definitions, analysis populations, imaging assessment and missing-data methods. Matched within-cohort threshold contrasts were calculated without pooling. No confidence intervals were estimated.

Results

Nine trial families contributed 46 endpoint records; four families yielded five contrasts. In the MEASURE-1 (n=168) and ENRADAS (n=69) imaging cohorts, changing the modified Stoke Ankylosing Spondylitis Spine Score (mSASSS) threshold from change ≤0 to ≤2 increased reported non-progression from 60.7% to 82.1% and from 52.2% to 72.5%, respectively (differences, 21.4 and 20.3 percentage points, calculated from rounded rates). These cohorts shared a reading campaign. SELECT-AXIS-1 (≤0 versus <2) differed by 13.2 percentage points; denominator linkage was inferred. PREVENT (≤0.76 versus ≤2) yielded count-derived differences of 1.4 and 2.2 percentage points across two sequences. No lesion-specific repair endpoint was identified in the inspected material.

Conclusions

Reported non-progression proportions varied with endpoint definition within the same cohort. Explicit thresholds, analysis populations and imaging methods are therefore necessary for interpretation. These descriptive contrasts do not estimate comparative treatment efficacy or demonstrate structural repair. Restricted retrieval and incomplete registry and supplementary-source assessment limit generalizability.

Review status: internal AI-assisted source checks; no external peer review.

One finding, read carefully

A different cutoff.
The same participants.

In the MEASURE-1 comparative reading dataset, the reported rate changes from 60.7% at score change ≤0 to 82.1% at ≤2. The 21.4 percentage-point difference subtracts rounded published rates. It measures a classification difference, not a treatment benefit.
