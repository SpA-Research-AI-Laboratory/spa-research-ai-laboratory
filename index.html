01 / Understanding outcomes

What do we mean
by “better”?

Start with the question a study is actually asking.

Schematic explanation

Disease activity & symptoms

How someone feels.
What a study measures.

Studies can measure symptoms, disease activity and inflammatory signals. Feeling better alone does not establish that inflammation has decreased. These are also different questions from whether spinal structure changes.

Structural progression

Looking for additional
change over time.

A study can look for additional structural change over time. “No progression” still depends on what was measured and the threshold used.

Structural repair

Asking about damage
that already exists.

Repair asks whether an existing structural lesion itself has regressed. Prevention of new damage does not, by itself, answer that question.

These are different research outcomes. One does not automatically establish another.

02 / Our first completed study

A small phrase can hide
a big difference.

When a report says “no progression”, its exact definition matters.

Our first completed pilot examined how accessible trial reports described roughly two years of spinal structural change. We assembled records from nine trial families and found that the definitions need to be read alongside the populations, readers and missing-data rules.

Conceptual contrast · thresholds are not interchangeable

“No increase”, “below a cutoff” and “below measurement error” require different decision rules.

In four matched parent trial families, changing the threshold changed the reported proportion meeting a non-progression definition within the same cohort. The audit retains five separate contrast rows because PREVENT has two treatment sequences.

These are descriptive within-cohort comparisons. The thresholds and populations differ; the families are not independent methodological replications. The result supports careful interpretation, not a preferred threshold or a treatment ranking.

How we work

A question.
A source.
A visible limit.

A reproducible record is more useful than an impressive claim that cannot be checked.

01

Frame a bounded question

State the population, outcomes, eligibility criteria and stopping rule before interpreting findings.

02

Trace the original sources

Preserve definitions, analysis populations, source locations and access limits alongside extracted records.

03

Challenge the interpretation

Examine missingness, shared cohorts and alternative readings. Checks by AI agents are internal review. Specialist external review is a separate stage.

04

Release methods and limits

Provide the dataset, reproducible arithmetic and a versioned account of completed and deferred checks.

An open methodological question

The next useful question
is a careful one.

What would a study need to show before calling a structural change “repair”?

Preventing additional structural damage and demonstrating regression of an existing lesion are distinct research questions. No lesion-specific repair endpoint was identified in the structural material inspected for this pilot; that does not establish its absence everywhere.

About SpA Lab

A small laboratory.
An open
research practice.

SpA Lab is an independent, AI-supported computational research project focused on axial spondyloarthritis.

Founded by Pawel Kuklik, it uses bounded questions, source-linked records and reproducible analysis to make evidence easier to inspect.

Its first output is a descriptive methods pilot. Internal AI review is part of the workflow; it is not external expert validation.
